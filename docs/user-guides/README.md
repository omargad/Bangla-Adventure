# Bangla Adventures user guides

These guides explain, step by step, how to use Bangla Adventures. There is one guide for each
kind of account. They are written for people who are not technical: teachers and parents at
community Bangla schools, the school's office staff, and any adult helping a child.

Each guide covers signing in, the everyday tasks for that role, switching between English and
Bangla, privacy and safety, troubleshooting and where to get help. Button names and messages
are written exactly as they appear on the screen, in **bold** for things you select or see as a
heading or label, and in *"italics with quotation marks"* for messages. The pictures use
made-up people and example addresses, and passwords and recovery codes are blurred.

## Which guide do I need?

| Role | Who it is for | Guide |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Student | Children learning at a community Bangla school, and the grown-up who sits with them | [Student guide](student.md) |
| Parent | Parents and carers who want to follow their own children's classes, results and attendance | [Parent guide](parent.md) |
| Teacher | Teachers who run classes, add students, set activities, take attendance, post announcements and write topics | [Teacher guide](teacher.md) |
| Administrator | The school's office staff or lead teacher who approves accounts and content and looks after every class | [Administrator guide](administrator.md) |

for teacher access.

## How accounts and approval work

**Signing in.** Students sign in with a username and a password of at least 8 characters.
Parents, teachers and Administrators are grown-up accounts: they sign in with an email address
and a password of at least 10 characters.

**Getting an account.** There are two ways.

- **Create your own** on the welcome page, with **Create student account**,
  **Create parent account** or **Request teacher account**. The account then waits in the
  Administrator's **Waiting for approval** list, and you cannot sign in until the
  Administrator approves it.
- **The school makes it for you.** An Administrator can create any account, or import parents
  and students together from one family list. A teacher can create student accounts from a
  CSV class list. Nothing is sent by email: you get a printed sheet with a temporary password,
  shown only once. At your first sign-in you choose your own password.

**Forgotten passwords.** Grown-ups get eight one-time recovery codes when they create an
account or choose their own password. There is no email reset: a recovery code is the way back
in. Students do not have recovery codes. A student who forgets their password, or a grown-up
with no codes left, asks the Administrator for a new temporary password.

**Parents and children.** A parent account shows nothing about a child until an Administrator
links the two, through **Family access** or the family import. A parent only ever sees the
children linked to them, and can look but not change anything.

**Classes.** A teacher, or an Administrator, adds students to a class by searching and ticking
their names or by uploading a CSV class list. Students do not need a class code.

**Content approval.** Students and parents only see new learning content after an
Administrator has checked and approved it. This covers topics, the 18 lessons, recordings of
real voices, lesson videos (a separate decision from the lesson's text), class activities and
class sources. Class lists, attendance and announcements do not need approval. On a new
installation all 15 topics and all 18 lessons start as waiting for approval. Only an
Administrator approves, and what an Administrator writes or uploads is published at once. An
Administrator can also switch off approval for teachers' topics; teachers then see on their
**Topics** page that their topics are published as soon as they save them.

**The first Administrator.** The first Administrator account is set up once, on the server, by
the person who runs it (see
[Setting up the first Administrator](administrator.md#setting-up-the-first-administrator)).
After that, Administrators can make other Administrators from the website.

## Words used in these guides

| Word | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Administrator** | The person at the school who approves accounts and content and looks after every class. |
| **Topic** | A short reading about the history, culture or literature of Bangladesh, in English and Bangla, often with a quiz. Topics are shared by the whole school. |
| **Lesson** | One of the 18 Bangla lessons, with the parts Overview, Listen, Read, Speak, Write, Watch and Mastery. |
| **Class activity** | A short multiple-choice quiz a teacher sets for one class. |
| **Class source** | A useful link a teacher shares with one class. |
| **Announcement** | A message from a teacher to a class, for everyone, students only or parents only. |
| **Temporary password** | A one-time password on a printed sheet. You replace it with your own at your first sign-in. |
| **Recovery code** | One of eight one-time codes a grown-up saves to reset a forgotten password. |

## Getting help

- **Students** ask their teacher first, and the Administrator (through their teacher or a
  grown-up) for accounts and passwords.
- **Parents** ask the Administrator about accounts, approval, linking and passwords, and their
  child's teacher about classwork, results, attendance and announcements.
- **Teachers** ask the Administrator about accounts, classes and approvals.
- **Administrators** ask another Administrator at the school, or the person who runs the server
  if the website is down, the only Administrator is locked out, or narration needs a key.

Never send a password or recovery code to anyone, including school staff.

For the people who install and run the website, the technical documents are listed in the
[handover deliverables index](../DELIVERABLES.md).
